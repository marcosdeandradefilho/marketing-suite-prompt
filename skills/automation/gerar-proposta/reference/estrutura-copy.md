# estrutura-copy.md — como escrever a proposta que fecha

> Grounded in proposal-writing practice (Proposify, Better Proposals, PandaDoc,
> Freshproposals, Qwilr, Alex Hormozi value framing). The PRINCIPLES below are consensus and
> well-sourced; specific conversion multipliers from vendor reports are DIRECTIONAL (the exact
> numbers sit behind registration and were flagged unverifiable) — use them to guide structure,
> never quote a precise "+X%" to the user as fact.

## The one lever that matters most: client-centric, not "sobre nós"
The single biggest reason proposals lose is **self-focused copy** — pages about how great the
freelancer/agency is before ever naming the client's problem. The fix: the first 1-2 pages are
about the CLIENT — their situation, their pain, their desired outcome. Use **"você / seu
negócio"**, not "nós / nossa empresa". Apply **PAS** (Pain → Agitate → Solve): name the concrete
pain, show its cost, then present the solution as the bridge. (Better Proposals: "the proposal
should be a mirror reflecting the client's pain and future success.")

Practical rule for this skill: if the diagnosis/contexto section says "nós" more than "você", rewrite it.

## Canonical section order (consensus across all sources)
Build in this order. For a freelancer service proposal, target **5-8 pages** of real content
(short, scannable proposals get read more; past ~15 pages completion drops). One section per
logical page where it helps the layout breathe.

1. **Capa** — empresa do freelancer (logo/nome) + "Proposta de [serviço] para [Cliente]" + data
   + validade da proposta (ex: "válida por 15 dias"). On-brand, high-contrast, one clear focal point.
2. **Resumo executivo (~100 palavras)** — in the client's words: the situation, what you'll do,
   the outcome, the investment in one line. A busy decision-maker should grasp the whole deal here.
   Customizing this section to the client is the highest-leverage edit in the whole document.
3. **Contexto / diagnóstico** — the client's current pain, concrete and specific. If the lead came
   from the Prospector, this is where the **"sinal de oportunidade"** goes (e.g. "seu site não é
   responsivo e perde os 65%+ de visitantes que chegam pelo celular"). PAS lives here. Their pain first.
4. **Solução proposta** — how you solve THAT pain. Tie each part of the solution back to a pain
   named in §3. Outcome-oriented ("você passa a ter X"), not feature-dump.
5. **Escopo — o que está incluso / o que NÃO está** — an explicit included/not-included list.
   The not-included list prevents scope creep and reads as honesty/professionalism. Bullet, scannable.
6. **Cronograma** — simple milestone timeline (etapa → entrega). No date promises you can't keep;
   use relative phases ("Semana 1: ...", "Etapa 2: ...") unless the user gave real dates. Anti-guru:
   no "resultados em X dias" guarantees.
7. **Investimento** — see "Apresentação de preço" below. Frame as investimento/resultado, not custo.
8. **Prova social** — ONLY if the user supplied real testimonials/cases/logos. Quote + attribution
   + concrete result. **Never invent this** — omit the whole section if there's nothing real.
9. **Garantia / reversão de risco** — see "Garantia" below. Removes the fear of saying yes.
10. **Condições comerciais** — forma e prazo de pagamento (Pix copia-e-cola + QR, parcelamento,
    entrada+parcelas), validade, o que acontece ao aprovar. See `reference/pix.md`.
11. **Próximo passo (CTA único)** — see "CTA" below.

## Apresentação de preço (pricing psychology)
- **Frame as investimento e resultado, não custo.** Connect the price to the outcome from §3-4
  ("o investimento pra resolver [pain] e destravar [outcome]"). (Hormozi value framing: value =
  dream outcome × likelihood ÷ (time + effort + sacrifice) — raise the top, lower the bottom.)
- **Single price OR 3 níveis (good-better-best).** Use **3 tiers** when the service naturally
  scales (ex: gestão de tráfego básico / completo / completo+criativos; site 1 página / institucional /
  institucional+blog). The middle tier is the target; the top tier anchors and makes the middle feel
  reasonable. Mark the recommended tier ("mais escolhido"). Use a **single clear price** when the
  scope is fixed and tiers would feel padded. Default: single price unless the service obviously tiers.
- **Make the price visually easy to find** — a clean pricing table/box, not buried in a paragraph.
  Payment friction at the finish line kills deals; keep the money + how-to-pay unmissable and simple.
- Always **R$**, formatted `R$ 1.500` / `R$ 1.500,00`. Never a bare `$`. No "renda garantida" framing.

## Garantia / reversão de risco
A specific, honest guarantee lowers the perceived risk of saying yes. Offer something real and
deliverable, e.g.:
- *"Se em [janela curta, ex: 7 dias] você não estiver satisfeito com [entrega concreta], a gente
  ajusta sem custo até ficar certo."*
- A scoped revision guarantee ("até 2 rodadas de ajuste inclusas").
Keep it truthful and within what the freelancer can honor. **Never** promise a business result
("garanto X vendas") — that's both anti-guru and unkeepable. Guarantee your WORK, not the market.

## CTA — próximo passo de foco único
End with ONE clear action, not "qualquer dúvida estou à disposição" (a weak CTA puts all the work
on the client and stalls the deal). Single-focus CTAs convert far better than competing options.
Tie it to the payment when possible:
- *"Pra começar: aprove esta proposta e garanta sua vaga pagando a entrada de R$ X no Pix abaixo —
  aí eu já inicio [primeira etapa] esta semana."*
- Or, if no deposit: *"Pra fechar, é só me responder 'aprovado' por aqui que eu te mando o contrato
  e a gente começa."*
Give the exact next step and make it feel easy.

## Tom
Professional, warm, confident — not stiff, not salesy. Active voice, "você". Brazilian PT-BR.
No jargon the client wouldn't use. No hype, no exclamation spam, no "incrível/imperdível". The
proposal should feel like a competent partner who understood the problem, not a pitch.

## Checklist anti-erro (rode antes de renderizar)
- [ ] Zero `[placeholder]`, zero `lorem`, nome do cliente escrito certo e por extenso.
- [ ] Primeiras páginas falam do CLIENTE (você), não do freelancer (nós).
- [ ] Preço em R$, fácil de achar, enquadrado como investimento.
- [ ] Escopo tem "o que NÃO está incluso".
- [ ] Garantia é real e sobre o trabalho, não sobre resultado de mercado.
- [ ] Nenhum depoimento/case/logo inventado (só o que o usuário deu).
- [ ] CTA único e concreto, ligado ao pagamento quando fizer sentido.
- [ ] Nenhuma promessa de prazo/resultado que não dá pra cumprir.
